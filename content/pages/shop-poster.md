# All wood dust is hazardous

It can cause asthma, allergies and nose cancer, and it burns.[^IARC-62][^HSE-WIS23] Fine dust you can't see matters most.[^HSE-WIS23]

## 1. Capture it at the tool

- [ ] Extraction on every dust-making machine, and switched on.[^HSE-WIS23]
- [ ] Sanders connected to an extractor.
- [ ] Gates open for the tool in use.

## 2. Protect yourself

- [ ] Fitted respirator (N95, P100 or FFP3), not a dust mask.[^HSE-WIS23][^NIOSH-FFR]
- [ ] Clean-shaven at the seal, or a loose hood PAPR.[^HSE-WoodDust][^NIOSH-PAPR]
- [ ] Seal check every time.[^NIOSH-FFR]
- [ ] Safety glasses or goggles.
- [ ] Shop clothes stay in the shop.[^NIOSH-TakeHome]

## 3. Clean up safely

- [ ] Class M or HEPA vacuum, or damp wipe.[^HSE-WoodDust][^HSE-WIS32]
- [ ] No brooms. No compressed air.[^HSE-WoodDust]
- [ ] Respirator on for cleanup.[^HSE-WIS23]

## 4. Prevent fires

- [ ] Nothing metal into the collector.[^HSE-WIS32]
- [ ] No dust buildup on ledges.[^HSE-WIS32]
- [ ] Oily rags dried flat or sealed in metal.[^CambridgeFD-2023]

## 5. Listen to your body

Cough, wheeze, runny nose or rash that eases away from the shop? See a doctor and tell them you work with wood.[^HSE-INDG172]
