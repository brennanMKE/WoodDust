# Site naming

Status: proposal, 2026-11-07. Not legal advice. **Check with a trademark lawyer before buying a domain you intend to brand on, and before filing any trademark application.**

## What the site is

A free, citation-backed reference that tells woodworkers which wood species' dust is hazardous and how to protect themselves (respirators, dust collection, cleanup). Audience: hobby and pro woodworkers, makerspaces, shop teachers. Tone: plain, calm, practical, a little personality. Mascots: Paul Bunyan and Babe the Blue Ox (owner's wish). Interim host: `wooddust.sstools.co`.

Naming goals: easy to remember, say and spell; clearly about wood dust or shop safety, or evokes it; works as .com or a reasonable alternate (.org, .info, .wiki, .tools, .shop); no implied medical or government authority; no fear words; no collision with woodworking brands.

## How the screening was done (and its limits)

- **Trademarks.** The USPTO's own search (tmsearch.uspto.gov) could not be queried from a script: the site is a JavaScript front end, the JSON endpoints behind it refused unauthenticated requests (HTTP 401/403/405), and Justia's trademark pages block automated fetches. Findings below therefore come from web search results that surface aggregator records (Justia, Markinton, TrademarkDir, TrademarkRegistration.app, TrademarkElite). Aggregators lag the register. **Confirm every mark cited here in USPTO TSDR before relying on it.** No state registers, common-law business names outside what web search found, or non-US registers (UKIPO, EUIPO, CIPO) were searched, except where noted.
- **Domains.** Checked via RDAP on 2026-11-07: Verisign for .com, Public Interest Registry for .org, Identity Digital for .info/.wiki/.tools/.shop, rdap.org as fallback. HTTP 404 is reported as "appears available"; HTTP 200 as "registered". RDAP says nothing about premium pricing or whether a registrar has a name reserved, so treat "appears available" as "worth checking at a registrar", not as a promise.
- **Classes considered.** Nice Classes 9, 16, 35, 41, 42 (software, publications, online information, education), 7 (dust collectors, vacuums), 8/20 (hand tools, furniture) and 9 (respirators/PPE).

## Top 3 recommendation

1. **Dust Almanac** — `dustalmanac.com` (+ .org to redirect). Best balance of memorable, sayable, and clean. "Almanac" promises a trustworthy reference without claiming medical or regulatory authority, and it matches the period look of public-domain Bunyan art, so the mascot feels native rather than bolted on. Clean trademark picture in web search; the phrase is distinctive enough that we could eventually register it. If "Dust" alone feels too broad, **Sawdust Almanac** is equally open and more obviously about wood.
2. **Sawdust Safety** — `sawdustsafety.com` / `.org`. The plainest option: it says what the site does in two words, ranks for the words people actually search, and nothing nearby conflicts. Trade-off: descriptive names are weak as brands and close to impossible to register as word marks, and "safety" sits a little closer to the "authority" tone we want to avoid. Good choice if search traffic matters more than brand.
3. **WoodDust** — `wooddust.org` (keep `wooddust.sstools.co` as the interim). Continuity with the repo and interim host, nothing to screen, zero learning curve. Trade-offs: `.com` is registered, and the term is fully generic so it can never be protected and will share search results with every "wood dust" page on the web. Reasonable if the owner prefers no brand at all.

Runner-up: **Sawdust Atlas** (`sawdustatlas.com`) if the almanac metaphor doesn't land.

**Mascots and the name should be decoupled.** Use Paul and Babe as illustrations and voice (e.g. "Babe says: sand with the collector on") under a non-Bunyan name. That gives the personality without inheriting the crowded Bunyan/Blue Ox trademark field. Avoid "Blue Ox" in the name; avoid "Dustwright" entirely.

## Paul Bunyan and Babe the Blue Ox: trademark findings

The folklore characters are public domain; nobody owns the names as such. But many businesses use them as marks, and the Paul Bunyan figure has a specific **lumber-industry trademark history**, which matters because our subject is wood.

Found (aggregator records, verify in TSDR):

- **Red River Lumber Co.** registered a Paul Bunyan head design as a trademark in 1921 (Reg. 290,330), drawn by W. B. Laughead ([bpmlegal](https://bpmlegal.com/wpaulbun.html), [tipurdy.org](https://tipurdy.org/?p=20651)). One secondary source says the mark passed to Kenneth Walker's Paul Bunyan Lumber Company in the 1940s. Current status unknown; may be dead. A face logo that copies Laughead's "Ol' Paul" head could still raise trademark (not copyright) questions if a lumber company uses a successor mark.
- **PAUL BUNYAN**, Reg. 0758707 (1963), expired after a missed Section 8 filing ([record](https://trademarkregistration.app/Trademark/Details/72155964)). Other PAUL BUNYAN word marks in textiles (Reg. 2976098, cancelled 2016) and Reg. 2071722 (cancelled 2004) ([record](https://trademarks.justia.com/751/25/paul-bunyan-75125013.html)).
- **PAUL BUNYAN LUMBERJACK SHOW**, Class 41 entertainment, registered 2014 (Florida operator). The owner sued a Wisconsin Dells show in 2015 ([Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2015/08/12/traveling-lumberjack-show-sues-wisconsin-lumberjack-show-over-paul-bunyan-trademark/), [BizTimes](https://biztimes.com/madison-dells-paul-bunyan-owners-say-theyre-honoring-heritage-and-was-no-intent-to-confuse/)). Neither side claimed "Paul Bunyan" alone. This shows the name's owners do litigate.
- **PAUL BUNYAN SCENIC BYWAY**, registered and renewed (2018) ([record](https://trademark.justia.com/785/54/paul-bunyan-scenic-78554777.html)).
- **PAUL BUNYAN** for plumbing services, Class 37, registered 2020 (Minnesota) ([owner](https://gleanmark.com/owner/paulbunyanplumbingllc)).
- **Paul Bunyan, Inc.** is listed with up to 24 filings across 19 classes; **Paul Bunyan LLC** (Venice, CA) filed **BUNYAN & BABE** in 2007 in Classes 16, 25, 28 and others for a film project; all shown as dead ([record](https://markinton.com/trademark/bunyan-babe-77226772), [owner](https://trademarkdir.com/owner/paul-bunyan-llc), [owner](https://trademarkdir.com/owner/paul-bunyan-inc)). Aggregator counts looked inconsistent; verify.
- **Unregistered but established users:** Paul Bunyan Communications (ISP, Bemidji MN), Paul Bunyan Land (Brainerd MN amusement park, 1950), Paul Bunyan Trail, Paul Bunyan's Cook Shanty (WI restaurants), Bemidji's 1937 statues (National Register of Historic Places).
- **Babe the Blue Ox:** no registration of the full phrase found ([search](https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Babe+the+Blue+Ox%22+trademark)). "BLUE OX" by itself is heavily registered: towing (Class 12, live), wood signs (Sawdust City LLC, Classes 19/35, live), spirits, rope, energy, and Blue Ox Millworks (woodworking business and school, Eureka CA; see shortlist row 12).

**Assessment.** Using Paul and Babe as *illustrated mascots* on an informational site, under a different name, is low risk: the characters are public domain and none of the marks found cover online woodworking-safety information. Using "Bunyan" *in the site name* is medium risk: not owned, but crowded, with a lumber-industry trademark lineage and at least one owner who has sued. Using "Blue Ox" in the name is medium-to-high risk because two live registrants (Sawdust City, Blue Ox Millworks) are in woodworking-adjacent fields, and Blue Ox Millworks runs a woodworking school, which is near our Class 41 education use. Do not draw a logo that resembles the Red River "Ol' Paul" head or the Blue Ox towing ox-head mark.

## Big woodworking brands to stay clear of

Avoid names that echo these, including sound-alikes and "Dust + [their word]" patterns:

- **Rockler** and its **Dust Right** line (Reg. 4093157, Class 7). Rules out Dustwright, Dust Rite, Dust Write.
- **Woodcraft** and its **Dust Connection** program ([press release](https://www.woodcraft.com/blogs/press-releases/dust-connection-branding-program)).
- **Oneida Air Systems** and **Dust Deputy** ([owner](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/oneida-air-systems-incorporated-1300094)).
- **Festool**, **SawStop**, **Grizzly**, **Powermatic**, **JET**, **Laguna**, **Woodpeckers**, **Bench Dog**.
- Publications and media: **Fine Woodworking** (Taunton), **Woodsmith**, **WOOD** magazine, **Woodworker's Journal**, **WoodNet**, **The Wood Database**, **The Wood Whisperer**, **Woodworking Network**, **LumberJocks**, **Sawmill Creek**.

## Imagery: what Bunyan/Babe sources are safe to draw from

Folklore is public domain; specific depictions are not automatically. US status as of 2026 (works published before 1931 are public domain; 1931–1963 works are public domain only if copyright was not renewed; 1964+ works are presumed protected):

- **W. B. Laughead / Red River Lumber Co. pamphlets, 1914–1922.** *Introducing Mr. Paul Bunyan of Westwood, Cal.* (1914), *Tales About Paul Bunyan, Vol. II* (1916) and *The Marvelous Exploits of Paul Bunyan* (1922) were all published before 1931, so their text and illustrations are **public domain in the US**. The 1914 booklet contains the first-ever drawing of Paul and the first drawing and naming of Babe ([Forest History Society interview](https://foresthistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Birth-Paul-Bunyan-1972.pdf), [Laughead papers](https://foresthistory.org/research-explore/archives-library/fhs-archival-collections/inventory-william-b-laughead-papers-1897-1958/)). The 1922 book is on [Project Gutenberg](https://gutenberg.org/files/32994/32994-h/32994-h.htm) with its illustrations. Red River did copyright the illustrations at the time ([Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/ad-man-turned-paul-bunyan-into-a-folklore-icon/601164742)), but those copyrights have expired. Caveat: the Laughead *head* was also a registered *trademark* (see above), so use the pamphlets for style reference, not as a logo.
- **Later Red River editions (1934, 1940, 1944 revisions).** Published 1931–1963; public domain only if not renewed. Red River dissolved in the 1940s so renewal is unlikely, but **unverified**. Treat as uncertain; prefer the pre-1931 editions.
- **Esther Shephard, *Paul Bunyan* (1924, illustrated by Rockwell Kent) and James Stevens, *Paul Bunyan* (1925).** Pre-1931: public domain in the US.
- **Bemidji statues (1937) and Brainerd's Paul Bunyan Land talking statue (1950).** Sculptures from the renewal era; status unverified. Also local trade-dress and tourism associations. Do not copy; photographing them for reference is fine, reproducing their likeness as our mascot is not.
- **Disney, *Paul Bunyan* (1958 animated short).** Disney renewed its catalog as a matter of course; **assume protected** (through at least 2053). Do not use the design, colors, or poses. No public-domain evidence was found ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Bunyan_(film))).
- **Anything after 1963** (children's books, Brainerd fiberglass Babes, Bunyan & Babe 2017 film, stock art): protected.

**Recommendation:** commission or generate **original** Paul and Babe art in our own style. Use the 1914–1922 Laughead pamphlets only as period reference (tall-tale proportions, lumber-camp setting, a blue ox), not as source images to trace or recolor. Give Paul a modern twist that serves the message: a fitted P100 half-mask, safety glasses, a dust collector hose on Babe's harness. Avoid the Red River head-logo composition, the Disney character design, and the Bemidji statue poses. Keep the art and the wordmark separate so either can change without the other.

## Passkeys are bound to the domain

The planned sign-in uses passkeys (WebAuthn). A passkey's relying-party ID is the registrable domain, so credentials created on `wooddust.sstools.co` (RP ID `sstools.co` or `wooddust.sstools.co`) **will not work** on `dustalmanac.com` or any other final domain. There is no migration API; browsers and authenticators won't present them.

Recommend one of:

1. **Pick and buy the final domain before opening sign-ups**, and serve the interim site from it (even as a redirect target), so every passkey is created against the final RP ID. This is the simple path.
2. If sign-ups must open on the interim host, **plan a re-enrollment path**: keep accounts keyed by a stable user ID with a verified email, and when the domain changes, send a one-time sign-in link that lets each user register a new passkey on the new domain. Budget for support questions and some account loss.

Also keep the API, OG images and canonical URLs on one domain from day one; a later move costs search ranking as well as passkeys.

## Lawyer line

Everything above is desk research from public web sources and aggregator trademark records; the USPTO register was not queried directly. **Before buying a domain to brand on, before publishing a logo, and before filing any trademark application, have a trademark lawyer run a clearance search (USPTO, state, common law, and UK/EU if the audience is there).**
