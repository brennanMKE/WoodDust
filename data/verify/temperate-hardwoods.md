# Verify list: temperate-hardwoods batch

Species: beech, birch, ash, alder, poplar, sweet-chestnut, black-locust. Researched 2026-11-07.

## Citations not fully verified or not read

- **Kespohl-2006** (black locust). Citation confirmed (J Allergy Clin Immunol 2006;118(2):522-524, PMID 16890782, erratum 118(5):1189). It is a research letter with no abstract, behind a paywall. We did not read the number of patients or whether specific inhalation challenge was done. Evidence level 1 rests on the title plus Schlunssen-2018's description (sIgE immunoblot; classed as occupational asthma). Needs full-text check.
- **Hernandez-1999** (beech rhinitis). Letter with no abstract. Test methods unread. Beech level 1 does not depend on it alone (DeZotti-1996 used provocation testing).
- **Scheman-1999** (white ash contact dermatitis). The one-sentence abstract gives no test method. Kept at level 3. Read the full text to confirm patch testing and upgrade to level 1 if so.
- **Wilhelmsson-1984** (birch). The abstract does not name species. The birch link comes only from Schlunssen-2018. Birch respiratory hazard kept at level 3.
- **Spiewak R, Bozek A, Maslowski T, Brewczynski PZ.** Occupational asthma due to wood dust exposure (ash, oak, beech and pine): a case study. Ann Agric Environ Med 1994;1:73-76. No PMID or DOI found (citation from the Schlunssen-2018 bibliography). Not cited. Could support beech/ash asthma if located.
- **Oertmann C, Bergmann KC.** Allergologie 1993;16:334-340, and **Kersten W, von Wahl P-G.** Allergologie 1994;17:55-60. German, not indexed in PubMed. Schlunssen-2018 cites them for ash and beech. Not cited.
- **Innocenti A et al.** Wood dust and nasal diseases: exposure to chestnut wood dust and loss of smell (pilot study). Med Lav 1985;76(4):317-320. PMID 4069066. No abstract. Not cited; could support a chestnut nasal effect.

## Claims and names

- **DeZotti-1996**: the abstract says "chestnut" and "acacia" without botanical names. Chestnut was assumed to be *Castanea sativa*. "Acacia" in Italy often means *Robinia pseudoacacia*. If the full text confirms that, add DeZotti-1996 to black locust as level 1 asthma confirmed by provocation testing.
- **Alder**: Schlunssen-2018 lists alder as occupational asthma, citing Ahman 1995 (PMID 8588687). That paper found only one positive alder RAST and concluded IgE allergy did not explain symptoms, so the table entry looks unsupported. No primary alder report was found. Alder has only HSE level 2 entries.
- **Birch**: no verified primary with provocation or sIgE for birch dust was found.
- **HSE-WIS30 beech row**: the note "(possibly from bark lichens)" may cover all listed effects, not only eye irritation. We read the text from another agent's copy of the archived PDF (web.archive.org snapshot 2020-02-12). The live hse.gov.uk/pubns/wis30.pdf URL returned 404. Its footer says it is licensed under the Open Government Licence.
- **Black locust is not listed in HSE-WIS30.**
- **ACGIH A4**: the A4 class for "all other wood dusts" was not added as `carcinogen_class` rows for ash, alder, poplar, sweet chestnut or black locust, because the brief only requires A1/A2. The agency agent should decide site-wide.
- **Image prompt species not checked on WFO**: *Betula alleghaniensis* (yellow birch), *Alnus rubra* (red alder). *Fraxinus americana* returned two candidate authorities on WFO matching (L. vs Marshall). All genus and main species names in the records were matched on WFO.
- **POWO** was blocked by a Cloudflare challenge, so all botanical names were checked on WFO instead.
